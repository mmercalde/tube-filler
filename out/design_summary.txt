1  THE JOB
==============================================================================
  post bore (square)                     70.1 x 70.1 bore  76.2 sq PTR x 3.05 wall x 6 m
  post internal volume                             29.5 L  = side^2 x length
  x 12 posts                                        354 L  
  + hose hold-up                                    4.1 L  (8 m of 1")
  grout to mix (10% waste)                          394 L  
  ! The brief assumed ~27 L/post.  76.2 sq PTR x 3.05 wall holds 29.5 L -- MORE, not less.
    The job is 354 L, not 324 L -- a difference of 30 L (42 kg of cement).
    Square PTR is the reason: a 76.2 square bore is 4914 mm2 against 3859 mm2 for round
    tube of the same nominal size -- 27% more, and it all has to be
    mixed.  Under-ordering cement stops you mid-post.

4  TORQUE BUDGET AND DRIVE MARGIN  -- 900 W DRILL
==============================================================================
  stator friction (ASSUMED)                      22.6 N.m  <-- A GUESS.  Measure it: test_plan W-4
  packed gland, run loose                         2.5 N.m  vs 5.0 at normal service preload
  feed auger                                      1.0 N.m  
  pressure @ 3.10 bar                             2.5 N.m  = dP.V/2pi
  TOTAL AT DUTY                                  28.6 N.m  (748 W at the rotor)

  drill continuous                               15.0 N.m  at 250 rpm
  drill burst / breakaway                        25.0 N.m  
  MARGIN ON CONTINUOUS                             0.53 x  want >= 1.30
  margin on burst                                  0.88 x  

  VERDICT: SHORTFALL AT THE ASSUMED FRICTION.  Read the next block before
  concluding the machine does not work -- the assumption, not the drill, is
  what is unproven.

  WHAT ACTUALLY DECIDES THIS -- the stator friction, which nobody has measured:

      stator friction |  total at duty |  drill cont.  |  drill burst  | stalls at
        [N.m]         |     [N.m]      |    15 N.m     |    25 N.m     |   [bar]
      ----------------+----------------+---------------+---------------+----------
                  3.0 |            9.0 |     OK        |     OK        |     23.2
                  5.0 |           11.0 |     OK        |     OK        |     20.7
                  6.5 |           12.5 |     burst     |     OK        |     18.8
                  8.0 |           14.0 |     burst     |     OK        |     17.0
                 10.0 |           16.0 |     burst     |     OK        |     14.5
                 15.0 |           21.0 |     burst     |     OK        |      8.2
                 20.0 |           26.0 |     no        |     no        |      1.9
                 22.6 |           28.6 |     no        |     no        |      0.0

  max stator friction, continuous                 9.0 N.m  (and only 7.0 with 1.30x margin)
  max stator friction, burst                     19.0 N.m  breakaway only
  ...if the gland is run tight                    6.5 N.m  the gland alone costs 2.5 N.m -- run it weeping

  So: the drill drives this pump if and only if the stator's running friction
  comes in at or below about 9 N.m.  That is not an unreasonable
  number for a wetted D6-3 at light interference -- and the fact that the vendor
  sells a DRILL-HEAD rotor variant says the manufacturer expects exactly this.
  But it is not proven.  Measure it on water (test_plan W-4) BEFORE mixing grout.

  Three levers, in order of effect, if the measured friction comes in high:
    1. BREAK-IN SPACERS.  Fit the 0.5 mm-crush spacer set and run cement milk
       for 30 min.  Interference torque falls as the rubber beds in.  This is
       the big one and it costs four pipe offcuts.
    2. Run the gland weeping, not dry.  Worth ~2.5 N.m.
    3. Shorten the hose and thin the mix.  Worth ~1 N.m -- least effect, because
       friction, not pressure, dominates this budget.
  If it still will not carry it: the upgrade path is 64 N.m at 193 rpm
  on the SAME hex stub -- see README section 11.

5b  THE CROSS-PIN BOSS -- the worst station, not the average
==============================================================================
  Section 5 is an average over the plain hub.  A screw meters on its WORST
  station.  On this screw that is the boss around each drive pin, and these
  numbers walk the real core profile end to end at 0.25 mm.

  boss swept OD x axial                        48 x 30 mm  lens, 9 mm fairing each end
  boss plan form                               48 x 44 mm  major axis on the pin; flush with the hub at 90 deg
  local conveyed annulus at the boss             1219 mm2  vs 1508 open hub = 81%
  MIN LOCAL OVERFEED, all z                        1.25 x  gate 1.15; worst at z = -6.0 mm  PASS
  free area at the boss                               88%  of the plain-hub section; gate 75%  PASS
  largest boss the gate allows                    49.2 mm  fitted at 48

  WAS a plain cylinder at 56 x 22.  That is 61% of the open-hub free area and
  a LOCAL overfeed of 0.58 -- the screw metered 1.54x the stator's swallow
  everywhere except the three stations where it mattered, and starved there.
  A screw that starves anywhere starves: the pump sees the pinch, not the mean.

  Two changes, doing two different jobs:
    AREA  -- swept OD 48, capped by the 1.15x gate above, which bites
             at 49.2 mm.  (A 50 mm boss reads 1.09x and does NOT clear it.)
    SHAPE -- a faired lens, so it sheds instead of damming: double-cone
             axially into a short land, and in plan an ellipse with its major
             axis on the pin, blended flush into the hub at +-90 deg.  The
             two quarters of the channel that carry no pin load stay at full
             hub diameter.  The gates above take NO credit for the lens --
             they are computed on the circle it sweeps.

  CROSS PIN -- 6 mm steel bearing on printed PETG, allowable 40 MPa:
    wall each side of the shaft                   6.50 mm  projected bearing area 78 mm2, both walls
    at duty (1.0 N.m)                            0.62 MPa  margin 65 x  (want >= 3)  PASS
    if it JAMS (25 N.m burst)                    15.4 MPa  margin 2.6 x
    steel pin, double shear at jam                 13 MPa  vs ~400 for a spring roll pin -- not the limit

  The duty case is the gate and it passes by 65x.  The jam
  case sits at 2.6x, and that is deliberate: a jam is bounded by
  the drill stalling, and the boss is the cheapest thing in the chain to give
  way.  If it does, the segment spins free on the shaft, the pump starves and
  you notice -- the pin stays captive in the shaft and nothing loose enters
  the barrel.  Reprint the segment; it is already a declared consumable.
  Note the two constraints have nearly converged: the overfeed gate caps the
  boss at 49.2 mm, and carrying a full-burst jam at 3x would want
  about 49.8.  There is no boss diameter that does both, which is worth
  knowing before anyone thickens it again.

7  STATOR CLAMP -- tie rods over hard-stop spacers
==============================================================================
  gasket seating force                             5503 N  3 mm elastomer, y=1.4 MPa
  separating force @ 6 bar                         1299 N  
  minimum total clamp                              6802 N  = 1701 N/rod = 3.4 N.m
  RECOMMENDED preload                         10000 N/rod  = 20 N.m dry on M10 8.8
  rod stress at recommended                       172 MPa  vs 640 MPa proof (8.8)
  spacer stress                                    46 MPa  3/4" sch40, 217 mm2
  spacer cut length                              268.0 mm  = stator_len - crush; HARD STOP
  The spacers, not the rubber, carry the preload.  This is the whole point:
  you cannot over-crush the stator no matter how hard you pull the rods, and
  over-crushing is what kills a PC stator (bore closes, torque spikes, burns).

  length / eccentricity                              31.1  want >= 20

  hinge; the rotor axis precesses on a cone, which needs 2.  Parallel forks
  will bind and tear the rotor head off.  Check this on assembly by hand.

9  STALL, OVERPRESSURE AND WHAT PROTECTS THE MACHINE
==============================================================================
  drill stalls the pump at                        0.0 bar  -- it cannot turn it at all

      stator friction [N.m] |  pump stalls the drill at [bar]
      ----------------------+--------------------------------
                        3.0 |                       23.2
                        5.0 |                       20.7
                        6.5 |                       18.8
                        8.0 |                       17.0
                       10.0 |                       14.5
                       15.0 |                        8.2

  This is the drill's one clear win over the motor it replaced.  A 1.5 kW motor
  on a 7.5:1 worm does not stall until roughly 140 bar -- twenty times the
  6 bar these joints are built for.  A 900 W drill stalls far sooner, and at
  low measured friction it stalls inside or near the design envelope.  The
  drive is now part of the protection instead of a liability.

  It is still not ENOUGH protection.  Keep all of it:
    1. 0-16 bar glycerin gauge on the discharge head, red band at 6 bar.
    2. 1" manual bypass ball valve back to the hopper.  It is the relief valve.
    3. Hose rated >= 40 bar working, crimped ends, whip checks.
    4. 30 mA RCD on the supply.  A hand drill in a wet steel machine has no
       earthed frame of its own and no overload relay.  The RCD is the only
       electrical protection on this build -- do not omit it.

  AND the shutdown rule, which is different from the motor version:
    CLOSE THE DISCHARGE VALVE, THEN RELEASE THE TRIGGER.  In that order.
    A drill freewheels when you let go -- there is no gearbox holding anything.
    The 1.24 bar column in the post will drive the rotor backwards and
    drain itself into the hopper, and you will not see it happen.
